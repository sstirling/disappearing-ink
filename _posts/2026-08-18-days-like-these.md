It seemed like a cruel trick.

How could an otherwise healthy person who was diagnosed with Alzheimer’s disease in July be facing cancer in August?

But there it is, at least in test results. No matter how creatively I Google search, no matter what AI assistant I use to analyze the scientific jargon delivered to me, I get the same result.

“The results presented describe an active case of multiple myeloma.”

I don’t know this for sure yet. I think it’s worth pausing here to note that.

In America today, test results are often provided to patients long before an experienced professional can explain them. Patients and their families are left to the purgatory of waiting. Everyone I know who has been in this situation fills this space with internet searches.

It begs the question: Why are we doing this?

Fortunately, because of her Alzheimer’s, Carol has lost the ability to remember most passwords and doesn’t know how to log into her online healthcare portal.

That’s my blessing for the moment.

Most days, Carol has forgotten she has Alzheimer’s, though she acknowledges she has memory issues. The last time I brought it up, she became furious with me and hung up the phone.

There’s no value in me trying to explain test results. So we wait.

Because of the battery of tests she’s undergoing, Carol seems to remember she’s undergoing tests for cancer. Right now that’s a win. I haven’t really started to wrap my head around what this could mean for us, but there’s time. This has to pass for good enough, today.
